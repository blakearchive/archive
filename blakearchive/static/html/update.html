30 November 2026

The William Blake Archive is pleased to announce the publication of five etchings and engravings by William Blake: Christ Trampling on Satan, Lear and Cordelia, Two Afflicted Children, Two Views of an Afflicted Child, and Coin of Nebuchadnezzar and Head of Cancer. With these publications we have completed the category of Separate Prints and Prints in Series, Designed by Blake and Engraved by Other Engravers.

The production of all but the last of the plates named above is associated with the family of Blake's patron, Thomas Butts (1757-1845). Early in 1806, Blake began to teach drawing and engraving to Butts son, Thomas Butts, Jr. (1788-1862). As receipts indicate, payment for these lessons extended from 3 March 1806 to 14 January 1808, but instruction might have continued a little longer. Although unsigned, the history of the copperplate of Christ Trampling on Satan and the provenances and inscriptions on several impressions of Lear and Cordelia firmly associate these works with the Butts family and the skills young Thomas learned from Blake. Both are based on extant drawings by Blake. The preservation of four states of Lear and Cordelia allows us to trace the development of the plate from early proof to finished (or almost finished) print. These are presented in the Archive as Copies 1 through 4.

Many of the hatching strokes in these prints indicate the work of an amateur, but their attribution to the younger Butts is complicated by two factors. We can expect that a drawing and engraving instructor would participate directly in a new pupil's early efforts. It would not be surprising if Blake outlined the major features of the design on the copperplate and perhaps cut a few demonstration strokes as part of his lessons. Thus, all of the plates attributable to Butts Jr. may have touches from Blake's own hand, however slight. Further, there is a Butts family tradition that the father profited more than the son from Blake's lessons. We are unable to determine if the elder Butts added his own efforts to these plates.

Although Two Afflicted Children and its similar companion, Two Views of an Afflicted Child, look like illustrations for a medical text, the history of the only known impressions associate them with the Butts family. The evidence for Blake's involvement in designing these odd images is admittedly slight, but they are included in the Archive for the sake of completeness and in the hope that further research will clarify their attribution.

The two images in Coin of Nebuchadnezzar and Head of Cancer, known only in a single unsigned impression probably engraved by John Linnell, are definitely attributable to Blake as their designer. The profile is based on one of Blake's Visionary Heads, drawn c. 1819, and is in part a self-portrait. The engraving, however, is associated with the production of illustrations for John Varley's A Treatise on Zodiacal Physiognomy, published in 1828 and also available in the Archive. Varley planned to issue further installments of his booklet, but only the first was published. Coin of Nebuchadnezzar and Head of Cancer was very probably intended for inclusion in one of these unpublished volumes.
